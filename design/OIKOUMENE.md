## 0. The pitch

> *Hecataeus of Miletus drew the world as a round shield: three lands around one sea, and a river running round the rim. In the middle he put a stone. Everything past Aethos is the rest of the shield.*

After the League, Aeolus's bag of winds blows the fog off the edge of the Aethos chart, and the *Euploia* sails onto a second one. **The Oikoumene** is the inhabited world as Herodotus heard of it: round, centered on the Omphalos, and ringed by the river-ocean Okeanos.

- **Ten lands, three doors.** Open the Bag at a fog bank on Aethos's edge. East lies the Hellespont and Troy, south the mouths of the Nile, west the Odyssey's sea. From those three ports the world opens: Lydia's gold and the Gordian Knot; Colchis, the Amazons and the White Isle across the Black Sea; the Scythian steppe and, beyond the North Wind, Hyperborea; the Nile up past the cataracts to Aithiopia and the Gate of Dawn; Libya's oases and its sea of sand; and the far west, where Atlas holds up the sky, the Hesperides keep their apples and Herakles set his Pillars. Round all of it runs **Okeanos**, with the Cimmerians' mist, the Land of Dreams, the Gorgons and the Isles of the Blessed.
- **The world after fate.** The Loom wove the whole world, so prophecy ended everywhere at once. Every land has a fate that just came undone. Cassandra has stopped seeing Troy burn. The Phoenix isn't due, and nobody knows if it's coming. The Hyperboreans, who always lived exactly a thousand years, have no idea how long they have, and they're delighted. Atlas would like to know if he can put the sky down now.
- **Every other Pokémon lives here.** The 152 Aethos species are the main game's dex. The other 1,025 National Dex species live in the Oikoumene, each in a land that fits its type or its myth: ice in the north, dragons in Colchis, Ghost in the Cimmerian mist, Fairy and Grass on the Isles of the Blessed, Fighting and Steel on the plain of Troy.
- **A spine: the Periodos.** Alethea, Keeper of Records, wants the first true map of the world. "Truthfully, every map I've seen is a better story." Each land has a champion, most of them heroes of myth on their own ground: Hektor at the Skaian Gate, Hippolyta on the Thermodon, Memnon in Meroë, Helen, who sat out the whole war in Egypt. Beat all ten, and Herakles waits at his Pillars, where the world says *no further*.
- **The top of the curve.** Wild 60–80, trainers 70–85, legendaries 70–80, and the strongest at 90–100 (`BALANCE.md`).

## 1. The world as the Greeks drew it

**The shape.** The earliest Greek world maps were round. Herodotus laughed at them for it ("they draw Okeanos running round the earth, which is circular as if drawn with compasses," 4.36), and that laugh is the brief: the Oikoumene is the world Greeks imagined, drawn as they drew it. The chart is a disc.

- **Three lands round one sea.** Europe to the north, Asia to the east, Libya (Africa) to the south. The Greeks argued about whether Egypt belonged to Asia or to Libya, and the game lets the townsfolk argue too.
- **The middle.** Zeus sent eagles from the two ends of the earth, and they met over Delphi. The chart's center is the Omphalos, with Aethos as a small inlay around it.
- **The rim.** Okeanos is a river, not a sea: it flows round the world, and every river and spring rises from it. Beyond it lie the places the Greeks put at the edge of the known: the Gorgons, Geryon's red island, the house of Night, the Isles of the Blessed.
- **The sun's road.** Helios drives east to west by day. At night he sails home round Okeanos in a golden cup (Mimnermus, fr. 12; Stesichorus, the *Geryoneis*). That cup is the chart's fast road round the rim (§2).

**When is it?** Aethos keeps every myth's place but not its calendar, and the Oikoumene does the same. Troy stands, rich and unburned. The Argo hasn't come for the Fleece. Odysseus is at Priam's court on an embassy he'd rather not be on. Prophecy said all of it would end badly. Nobody can say that now, which is the point.

## 2. Getting there, getting back, and the order of play

### The fog at the edge of the chart

- The sea past the Aethos chart's edges is a fog bank that never lifts. Sailors say there's no wind out there to lift it; Aeolus keeps every wind in a bag.
- **Aeolus's Bag** (SYSTEMS §1, relic 13: "Defog; sail the fog banks") comes from Aeolus in the Odyssey arc. `ODYSSEY.md` owns that quest and its Tornadus.
- Open the Bag at one of three **fog banks** on Aethos's chart edge, and the winds tear the fog open. The *Euploia* sails through onto the Oikoumene chart.
- In the *Odyssey* the crew opened the bag by accident and were blown away from home (*Odyssey* 10.46–55). Here you do it on purpose.

```text
The fog stands up off the sea like a wall.

Open Aeolus's Bag?
```
?? YES/NO
```text
Every wind in the world comes out at once.

The fog doesn't lift. It runs.
```

### The three doors

| Door | Fog bank on the Aethos chart | First port in the Oikoumene | Land | Wild band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **East** | North of the Aegean, past the unnamed northern isles | **Sestos**, on the Hellespont | Thrace and the Troad | 60–66 |
| **South** | South of Knossa, past the Cretan Sea | **Naukratis**, in the Nile delta | Egypt | 60–67 |
| **West** | West of the Ionian islands | **Kroton**, on the Western Sea | The Western Sea (the Odyssey's waters) | 62–68 |

- All three open at once. The player picks.
- Each first port has the same welcome: an Asclepeion, an Agora, a fisher who gives the **Super Rod** to whoever arrives first (the other two give a Big Pearl), and Alethea on the quay with a blank chart (§5).
- `AETHOS_MAP.md` §Decisions #9 leaves the northern isles and the Ionian islands unnamed. The fog banks sit past them, so the map agent revising that file loses nothing.

### What opens what

The Oikoumene is open-ended. Levels steer the player outward (§9); the gates below only make sure each land is earned.

| Into | From | The gate | What it uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lydia | The Troad | None: the Royal Road runs east from Ida | — |
| The Euxine | The Troad | **The Clashing Rocks** at the Bosporus. Release Phineus's dove and sail through the gap behind it (§4.3) | Poseidon's Conch, a timing puzzle |
| Scythia | The Euxine | Sea lane from Leuke to Olbia | Poseidon's Conch |
| Hyperborea | Scythia | **The Rhipaean ice walls.** Carry a flame up from Olbia's hearth to melt them (SYSTEMS §2, the carried flame). Then through the Cave of Boreas | Hecate's Torch, a carried flame |
| Aithiopia | Egypt | **The cataracts** of the Nile | Horn of Achelous |
| Libya | Egypt | **The Great Sand Sea** is a maze; Ammon's ravens guide you (§4.7) | A key item from Ammonion |
| The Far West | Libya or the Western Sea | **The Pillars' current.** Sail against it | Aeolus's Bag |
| Okeanos (west) | The Far West | Past the Pillars, the ocean opens | Poseidon's Conch |
| Okeanos (north) | The Western Sea | The Cimmerian fog, a second fog bank | Aeolus's Bag |

No gate needs a time of day, a festival or luck. Helios's Cup sails by night, and a xenia host can rest you until night (SYSTEMS §10).

### The order a player usually lives it

1. **The Odyssey** begins after the League (`ODYSSEY.md`); Aeolus gives the Bag.
2. **A door** (60–66). Most players take the one nearest the story they're following: east for Troy, south for Egypt, west to keep sailing the Odyssey.
3. **The near lands** (60–70): the Troad, Egypt, the Western Sea, then Lydia and Libya.
4. **The far lands** (68–78): the Euxine, Aithiopia, the Far West, Scythia.
5. **The edge** (75–80): Hyperborea, then Okeanos and the Isles of the Blessed.
6. **The Pillars:** Herakles (§5).

### Getting back

- **By sea.** The fog stays torn. Sail back through any door at any time.
- **By Wings.** Daedalus's Wings fly within the chart you're on (the engine filters fly targets by chart, `CreateFlyDestIcons`, `src/region_map.c` line 2385). Each chart gets one **crossing target**:
  - on the Aethos chart, a cell in the fog named THE OIKOUMENE, which lands you at your last Oikoumene port;
  - on the Oikoumene chart, the Aethos inlay at the center, named AETHOS, which lands you at Akti.
  - Cost: two map sections and two heal-location entries (§10).
- **Helios's Cup.** Once lent, the Cup sails round Okeanos from any Okeanos shore at night, always westward round to the east. It is the rim's fast road, and the only way to reach the Isles of the Blessed.

### Helios's Cup

- **Where:** the Gate of Dawn, Aithiopia's eastern edge, where Helios stables his horses.
- **How:** Lampetie, Helios's daughter, keeps the stables. She lends the Cup's night passage to anyone who has watched the sun rise from the Gate itself. Arrive before dawn, stand on the stable roof as the sun comes up, and she hands you the Cup's rope.

```text
LAMPETIE: Father sails home in it every night. He won't notice a passenger.

LAMPETIE: He won't notice anything. He's asleep in the bottom of it.
```

- **Coordination:** `LABORS.md`'s tenth Labor, Geryon's cattle, is where Herakles borrowed the Cup in myth (Apollodorus 2.5.10). If the Labor wants it, Lampetie can lend it there too.

## 3. The chart

A black-figure disc on terracotta, in the same family as the Aethos chart (`AETHOS_MAP.md`, "How it is painted"). The land is reserved clay, the sea black glaze, and Okeanos is a band of wine-dark waves round the rim, painted with the Shield of Achilles's own device: a river running round the edge of the world (*Iliad* 18.607–608). The four corners outside the disc are terracotta, with a meander border.

The grid is the same 28×15 as Aethos's, so the engine's cursor code carries over untouched (§10). `~~` is Okeanos, `::` land, blank the inner seas, `[]` the Aethos inlay. Two letters mark a town, `**` a dungeon or sanctuary.

- **True to the Greek picture.** The Black Sea is up and right, Egypt down and right, Libya along the bottom, the Pillars at the left. The far north and far west are squeezed against the rim, as they were on the old maps.

## 4. The lands

Every map follows `MAP_DESIGN.md`. In particular:

- **Routes** have forced grass across the main path (two or three bands on these long routes), an easy first trainer in plain view, mandatory trainers at the narrows, a set piece, a rest point on long routes, signs at each end, and nothing in a dead end but a reward.
- **Every house** has a door, a unique interior and someone in it with something to do. The tables below give each household its line, and no interior repeats a layout.
- **Seams.** Lands never share a walking seam. They meet at harbors, passes (warps) and sea lanes, so each land gets its own secondary tileset without breaking MAP_DESIGN §5's seam rule. Maps inside one land share that land's secondary tileset and are exempt.
- **Herms.** Greek colonists carried Hermes with them, and so do the foreigners in their own ways. Each route's great waystone is a herm on Greek ground and the local equivalent elsewhere: an ibis-headed pillar of Thoth in Egypt (Herodotus's Hermes), a stone *baba* idol on the steppe, a cairn of Ammon in Libya. Anointing works the same (SYSTEMS §4) and they join the Wings' fly map.

Each land gives: its fate undone, its band, its towns and houses, its routes and dungeons, its champion, its land quest, and which legendaries it hosts (LEGENDS.md has the quests).

### 4.3 The Euxine: Colchis, the Amazons and the White Isle

> **The fate undone.** Achilles was offered a short glorious life or a long forgotten one. The offer is off. He's visiting his mother to talk it over.

- **Band:** wild 67–72, trainers 74–78, champion 76–78.
- **Look:** a dark green sea under low cloud, the Caucasus white along the east, the Phasis's pheasant-reeds, Aia's palace of bronze. The White Isle is bone-white rock and seabirds.
- **Music:** *The Black Sea*, Phrygian mode, low strings and a rowing beat in 9/8.

**The Clashing Rocks.** The Bosporus is guarded by the Symplegades, two cliffs that slam together on anything that passes (*Argonautica* 2.549–606).
- In the old days you asked the blind seer Phineus what to do. Now he can't see the future, but he remembers what worked once: release a dove, and sail through behind it while the rocks are open.
- **The puzzle.** A timing gate built on the Labyrinth's bull-leap special (`Ichor_BullLeap`, LABYRINTH §3). Release the dove; the rocks close on its tail feathers; press A as they draw back. Fail forward: the rocks bounce you out and the window widens each try.
- Once you've passed, the rocks stay open, as they did after the Argo.

```text
PHINEUS: I can't tell you if it'll work. That's new.

PHINEUS: I can tell you what worked once. Take the dove.
```

**Towns.**

|---|---|---|

## 5. The Periodos: the Oikoumene's spine

Hecataeus of Miletus wrote the first geography, the *Periodos Ges*, "Journey Round the Earth." Herodotus wrote his history half to correct it, and his method was *autopsy*, seeing for yourself. The Oikoumene's spine is the same argument the main story made: the truth against the better story.

**The setup.** Alethea meets you on the quay of whichever port you reach first. She is Keeper of Records now (STORY, post-game continuity), and she has brought a blank chart.

```text
ALETHEA: Every map of out here has a monster in the corner. Some have six.

ALETHEA: Truthfully, every map I've seen is a better story.

ALETHEA: So we're making one. You go and look. I write down what you saw.
```

**How it works.**
- The chart has ten panels, one per land. A panel fills when you beat that land's champion and visit its landmark. Alethea writes what's true and what was only told: the Arimaspians have one eye each (true: they squint); the Pygmies fight cranes (true); the Atarantes have no names (true); the gold-guardians are birds (false: they're bones).
- At Miletos, Hecataeus himself checks her work and is embarrassed by his own. "I said the Nile flowed from Okeanos. Did it?" "No." "…Put that in."
- **The Scroll of Deeds** tracks the panels.
- **The chart's art changes.** The Oikoumene region map ships with monsters drawn in its unfinished corners. Each completed land wipes its monster off and paints what you saw. Stretch: a second copy of the chart art, about 15 KiB (§13).

**The end: Herakles at the Pillars.** With all ten panels filled, and `LABORS.md`'s twelve done, the Herakleion's priest at Gadeira sends you to the strait. Herakles stands on Calpe, the northern Pillar, which he set up himself with NE PLUS ULTRA on it: no further.

```text
HERAKLES: Twelve labors. Somebody did mine over again, I hear. With a boy from Lakonia.

HERAKLES: I put these here so nobody'd go past. Nobody listened. Show me why.
```

- **The battle:** Herakles, Lv 86–88, six Pokémon, the Oikoumene's hardest trainer (§6).
- **After:** he scratches out NE and leaves PLUS ULTRA, "further," and Alethea's chart is finished. Reward: the trainer card's last stamp, the Periodos itself as a wall map in your room at home, and Herakles's club: the **War Horn's** upgrade to rematch every Oikoumene champion daily (SYSTEMS §3, Ares gift II).

### The rules

1. **One home each.** Every species has a home: the area where it is most common. Areas then fill their remaining slots with "shares" from neighboring areas of the same land and type, so no map feels thin (`COVERAGE.md`, the area table).
2. **Evolved forms in the wild.** BALANCE.md's ruling holds: at levels 60–80 a line appears as the stage it reaches by plain level-up. Butterfree is wild; Caterpie is in a Nursery or bred. Lines that evolve another way (stones, trade, friendship, moves) appear at the last stage before that, and the player evolves them.
3. **Type and myth.** A species goes where its types live first (`TYPE_HOMES` in the script). About 290 species are placed by hand for a myth (the `OVERRIDES` table): Apollo Smintheus's mice at Chryse; Proteus's seals and Ditto on Pharos; the cats of Bubastis and the sacred crocodiles in Egypt; the gold-digging ants and their anteaters on the Arimaspian heights; Herodotus's salt hills full of Garganacl; the Hercules beetle on Herakles's island; the scarab rolling the sun on the Pyramid Field; Stonjourner as the Hyperboreans' walking round temple.
4. **The user's examples are the backbone:** Ice in the north; desert Ground and Psychic in Egypt and Libya; dragons in Colchis; Fairy and Grass on the Isles of the Blessed; Ghost in the Cimmerian mist; Fighting and Steel at Troy.

## 10. The region map in the engine, and a feasibility note

**The short version: the expansion already runs two regions, Hoenn and Kanto with the Sevii Islands. Ichor can make the Oikoumene the second one by taking over Kanto's slot.** Feasibility: high. The work is data and art, plus about five small code edits.

### What the engine does today

| Piece | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Regions | `include/constants/regions.h` | An `enum Region` with `REGION_KANTO`, `REGION_HOENN` and the rest |
| Region of a section | `GetRegionForSectionId`, `include/regions.h` | Any map section from `KANTO_MAPSEC_START` (Pallet Town) to `MAPSEC_SPECIAL_AREA` counts as Kanto; everything else as Hoenn |
| Sub-charts | `GetKantoSubregion`, `src/regions.c` | Lists which Kanto sections belong to Sevii 1–3, 4–5 and 6–7, each with its own map art |
| Map art per chart | `gRegionMapInfos[]`, `src/region_map.c` around line 329 | One entry per `enum RegionMapType` (`REGION_MAP_HOENN`, `_KANTO`, `_SEVII123`, `_SEVII45`, `_SEVII67`, `include/region_map.h`): graphics, tilemap and palette for the Fly map and for the Pokédex area view |
| Which chart to show | `GetRegionMapType`, `src/region_map.c` line 1168 | Picks the chart from the player's current section |
| Section grid per chart | `GetMapSecIdAt`, line 1190 | Reads `sRegionMapSections_Kanto` (and the Sevii grids) or Hoenn's grid, by region |
| Fly targets | `sFlyLocations[]`, line 2186 | Each entry has a `regionMapType`; `CreateFlyDestIcons` (line 2385) draws only the current chart's |
| Post-game hiding | `IsIchorPostgameMapSec`, line 1225 | Ichor's own: hides Tainaron, Olympia and Atlantis until the game is cleared |
| Battle music by region | `src/pokemon.c` around line 5168 | Kanto trainers get a different theme |
| Regional evolutions | `IF_REGION`, `src/pokemon.c` line 4373 | Evolutions can check `GetCurrentRegion()` |

### The plan

1. **Reuse the Kanto section block.** Sections 88–192 (105 of them, Pallet Town to the Special Area) are Kanto's and Sevii's, and Ichor has no Kanto. Rename them to the Oikoumene's sections in `src/data/region_map/region_map_sections.json`. `GetRegionForSectionId` then calls every Oikoumene map Kanto, and every piece above follows without a code change: the right chart loads, the right grid reads, fly targets filter, the Pokédex area screen shows the right map.
   - The Oikoumene needs about 75 sections (§11). 105 is plenty, and the rest stay spare.
   - **Why not append new ones?** `MAPSEC_NONE` must stay below `0xFD` because met locations are a byte (`metloc_u8_t`, `include/gametypes.h`). With Aethos's 29 it is 241, leaving 11 (AETHOS_MAP.md, Limits). The Kanto block costs no headroom.
2. **Rename the region.** In `include/constants/regions.h`, keep the enum's slot and add `#define REGION_OIKOUMENE REGION_KANTO`, so Ichor's code reads clearly and upstream merges stay small. Kanto's name never appears to the player.
3. **The chart art.** Replace `graphics/pokenav/region_map/map_kanto.png` (and the Pokédex copy) with the Oikoumene disc, at the same limits as Aethos's: 8bpp affine, at most 256 tiles for the Fly map and 232 for the Pokédex, palette 7 (AETHOS_MAP.md, Limits). A round map with Okeanos's band repeats well, so the tile budget is realistic. Extend `tools/ichor/art/ui_world_map.py` to paint it and write the grid into `region_map_layout_kanto.h`, so art, grid and names stay in step.
4. **Sevii as sub-charts.** The Sevii mechanism is a ready-made zoom. Two candidates use it: the **Isles of the Blessed** (an inset chart past the rim) and the **Western Sea** if the Odyssey's islands need more cells than the main disc can give. Both are optional; the plan works with one chart.
5. **The crossing targets.** One fly target per chart for the other chart (§2): AETHOS on the Oikoumene's center, THE OIKOUMENE in Aethos's fog. Each is a section, an `sFlyLocations` entry and a heal location pointing across. **Low.**
6. **Music.** Point the `REGION_KANTO` branch at Ichor's Oikoumene trainer theme. **One line.**
7. **Map-name popups.** `src/map_name_popup.c`'s theme table skips the Kanto block: `LoadMapNamePopUpWindowBg` (around line 690) sends every Kanto-block section to the first entry's theme. Give the Oikoumene its own themes there, by land. **Low.**
8. **The post-game gate.** The Oikoumene's sections need no hiding: you can't reach its chart before the Bag.

### Honest risks

- **The region map's tile limit** is the tightest constraint. A disc with ten lands, a ring and an inlay is more detail than Aethos's chart. If it doesn't fit 232 tiles, use a Sevii-style sub-chart for the Western Sea or the north.
- **Kanto's hardcoded uses.** The expansion references Kanto sections in a few places (FRLG met-location names, `REGION_KANTO` music, `IF_REGION` evolutions). Renaming the block changes what those mean. No Ichor content depends on Kanto, but every reference should be grepped once when the block is renamed.
- **Upstream merges.** `region_map_sections.json` and the Kanto layout header will conflict on every upstream change to them. Keep the Oikoumene's edits in those two files and comment them.
- **Two charts make the Fly map a little more complex** for players. The crossing targets and the AETHOS inlay keep it legible.

## 13. Scope, ROM and decisions

### Scope

Every Pokémon must be obtainable eventually, so cutting a land means rerunning `coverage.py` with that land's areas removed; the script places every species somewhere else. That makes scope a dial, not a cliff.

| Tier | What | Outdoor maps |
|---|---|---|
| **1.0** (Phase 5, the post-game ships) | All ten lands at core size: the three doors and their ports; one hub town and three or four routes per land; the Clashing Rocks, the cataracts, the Sand Sea's ravens, Helios's Cup; all 78 encounter areas folded into those maps (dungeons keep their floors); the Nurseries; the Periodos's ten champions and Herakles; trade and breeding services; the chart, without the art swap | ~50 |
| **Stretch** | The rest of §4's towns and routes; the Golden Fleece as a full five-step quest; the Wagon Camp moving; the Labyrinth of Moeris; reversed prices at Meroë; the chart's art changing with the Periodos; the cross-chart Pokédex area view | ~75 |
