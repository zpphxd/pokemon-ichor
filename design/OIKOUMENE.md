## 0. The pitch

> *Hecataeus of Miletus drew the world as a round shield: three lands around one sea, and a river running round the rim. In the middle he put a stone. Everything past Aethos is the rest of the shield.*

After the League, the fog at the edge of the Aethos chart starts to move. Nobody was ever fated to sail past it, and now nobody is fated not to (ODYSSEY §1). **The Oikoumene** is the world beyond: the inhabited world as Herodotus heard of it, round, centered on the Omphalos, and ringed by the river-ocean Okeanos.

- **The post-game's epics carry you onto it.** Your own trireme, the *Euploia*, takes the Labors east to Thrace and Themiskyra and the Caucasus. Helios's golden cup takes them west to the Pillars, Erytheia and the Garden of the Hesperides. The Odyssey's storm off Cape Maleia throws you onto the wine-dark sea. And the same ship can sail south, any time, to the mouths of the Nile.
- **Ten lands:** Thrace and the Troad; Lydia's gold and the Gordian Knot; the Black Sea's Colchis, Amazons and White Isle; the Scythian steppe and, beyond the North Wind, Hyperborea; Egypt; Aithiopia up to the Gate of Dawn; Libya's oases and its sea of sand; the far west, where Atlas holds up the sky; the Odyssey's sea; and round all of it, **Okeanos**, with the Cimmerians' mist, the Land of Dreams, the Gorgons and the Isles of the Blessed. **Aeolus's Bag**, won in the Odyssey, blows away the last fog round the rim.
- **The world after fate.** The Loom wove the whole world, so prophecy ended everywhere at once. Every land has a fate that just came undone. Cassandra has stopped seeing Troy burn. The Phoenix isn't due, and nobody knows if it's coming. The Hyperboreans, who always lived exactly a thousand years, have no idea how long they have, and they're delighted. Atlas would like to know if he can put the sky down now.
- **Every other Pokémon lives here.** The 152 Aethos species are the main game's dex. The other 1,025 National Dex species live in the Oikoumene, each in a land that fits its type or its myth: ice in the north, dragons in Colchis, Ghost in the Cimmerian mist, Fairy and Grass on the Isles of the Blessed, Fighting and Steel on the plain of Troy.
- **A spine: the Periodos.** Alethea, Keeper of Records, wants the first true map of the world. "Truthfully, every map I've seen is a better story." Each land has a champion in a hall of the land's own, most of them heroes of myth on their own ground: Hektor at the Skaian Gate, Croesus in his treasury, Medea in her father's bronze palace, Memnon in Meroë, and Pharaoh Proteus in Memphis, who kept Helen through the whole war. Win all ten Emblems, and once the Labors are done, Herakles waits at his Pillars, where the world says *no further*.
- **The top of the curve.** Wild 60–80, trainers 70–85, legendaries 70–80, and the strongest at 90–95 (`BALANCE.md`), under Arceus's 95 (`POSTGAME.md`).

## 1. The world as the Greeks drew it

**The shape.** The earliest Greek world maps were round. Herodotus laughed at them for it ("they draw Okeanos running round the earth, which is circular as if drawn with compasses," 4.36), and that laugh is the brief: the Oikoumene is the world Greeks imagined, drawn as they drew it. The chart is a disc.

- **Three lands round one sea.** Europe to the north, Asia to the east, Libya (Africa) to the south. The Greeks argued about whether Egypt belonged to Asia or to Libya, and the game lets the townsfolk argue too.
- **The middle.** Zeus sent eagles from the two ends of the earth, and they met over Delphi. The chart's center is the Omphalos, with Aethos as a small inlay around it.
- **The rim.** Okeanos is a river, not a sea: it flows round the world, and every river and spring rises from it. Beyond it lie the places the Greeks put at the edge of the known: the Gorgons, Geryon's red island, the Land of Dreams, the Isles of the Blessed. `POSTGAME.md`'s Atlantis arc meets Okeanos too, at "the edge of the world" under Atlantis on the Aethos chart; it is the same river, seen from the other side of the disc.
- **The sun's road.** Helios drives east to west by day. At night he sails home round Okeanos in a golden cup (Mimnermus, fr. 12; Stesichorus, the *Geryoneis*). `LABORS.md`'s tenth Labor borrows the cup; after it, the cup is the rim's night road (§2).

**When is it? The songs and the people.** Aethos keeps every myth's place but not its calendar, and the Oikoumene does the same. The epics need it this way: Odysseus is home from his voyage long ago (ODYSSEY §1), and Achilles, whom the songs killed at Troy, is alive and undecided at Olympia (POSTGAME §3.1). So in Ichor the great songs and the people in them disagree, and the game plays the gap for what it is, the truth against the better story, which is the Periodos's own theme (§5).
- **Troy** stands on its hill. There was a war; the Trojans say it was smaller and shorter than the songs ("Ten years? It was a summer"), and Herodotus agreed that Helen was never there (2.118–120).
- **Hektor, Memnon and Achilles** are alive, and each has heard a song about his own death.
- **Odysseus** visits the plain every year to see how wrong the songs were. ODYSSEY's embellished Periplous is the same joke at sea.

### Getting back

- **By sea.** Every lane runs both ways, except the Odyssey's voyage itself, which is one way until you're home (ODYSSEY O1).
- **By Wings.** Daedalus's Wings fly within the chart you're on (the engine filters fly targets by chart, `CreateFlyDestIcons`, `src/region_map.c` line 2385). Each chart gets one **crossing target**:
  - on the Aethos chart, a cell in the edge fog named THE OIKOUMENE, which lands you at your last Oikoumene port;
  - on the Oikoumene chart, the Aethos inlay at the center, named AETHOS, which lands you at Akti.
  - Cost: two map sections and two heal-location entries (§10). LABORS's Abdera and Themiskyra become Wings landing points as their Labors already say.
- **Helios's cup.** After Labor 10, the cup docks at every Okeanos shore at night as well as at LABORS's Western Cape, and sails the rim west to east. It is the rim's fast road, and the only way to the Isles of the Blessed.

## 3. The chart

A black-figure disc on terracotta, in the same family as the Aethos chart (`AETHOS_MAP.md`, "How it is painted"). The land is reserved clay, the sea black glaze, and Okeanos is a band of wine-dark waves round the rim, painted with the Shield of Achilles's own device: a river running round the edge of the world (*Iliad* 18.607–608). The corners outside the disc are terracotta, with a meander border.

## 4. The lands

Every map follows `MAP_DESIGN.md`. In particular:

- **Routes** have forced grass across the main path (two or three bands on these long routes), an easy first trainer in plain view, mandatory trainers at the narrows, a set piece, a rest point on long routes, signs at each end, and nothing in a dead end but a reward.
- **Every house** has a door, a unique interior and someone in it with something to do. The tables below give each household its line, and no interior repeats a layout.
- **Seams.** Lands never share a walking seam. They meet at harbors, passes (warps) and sea lanes, so each land gets its own secondary tileset without breaking MAP_DESIGN §5's seam rule. Maps inside one land share that land's secondary tileset and are exempt.
- **Herms.** Greek colonists carried Hermes with them, and so do the foreigners in their own ways. Each route's great waystone is a herm on Greek ground and the local equivalent elsewhere: an ibis-headed pillar of Thoth in Egypt (Herodotus's Hermes), a stone *baba* idol on the steppe, a cairn of Ammon in Libya. Anointing works the same (SYSTEMS §4) and they join the Wings' fly map.
- **The epics' maps** in these lands keep their docs' designs, wild tables and levels. This file's maps sit round them.

Each land gives: its fate undone, its band, its towns and houses, its routes and dungeons, its champion, its land quest, and which legendaries it hosts (LEGENDS.md has the quests).

### 4.1 Thrace and the Troad

## 5. The Periodos: the Oikoumene's spine

Hecataeus of Miletus wrote the first geography, the *Periodos Ges*, "Journey Round the Earth." Herodotus wrote his history half to correct it, and his method was *autopsy*, seeing for yourself. The Oikoumene's spine is the same argument the main story made: the truth against the better story. ODYSSEY's Periplous (Odysseus's embellished sea-chart, which Alethea corrects) is the same idea for the wine-dark sea; the Periodos is it for the rest of the world, and the two charts join on Alethea's table.

**The setup.** Alethea meets you on the quay of whichever Oikoumene port you reach first. She is Keeper of Records now (STORY, post-game continuity), and she has brought a blank chart.

```text
ALETHEA: Every map of out here has a monster in the corner. Some have six.

ALETHEA: Truthfully, every map I've seen is a better story.

ALETHEA: So we're making one. You go and look. I write down what you saw.
```

If you've sailed the Odyssey already: "I did one for the sea. Now the rest."

**The ten halls.** Each land's champion keeps a hall in that land's own idiom: Hektor's Skaian Gate, Croesus's treasury, Aietes's bronze palace, Abaris's round temple, Pharaoh Proteus's precinct at Memphis, Memnon's bath-court, Kyrene's hippodrome, Arganthonios's mine gallery, Milo's palaestra and Rhadamanthys's tower of Kronos. Every hall has a puzzle built from its land's myth, three to five trainers, a scene, and a prize: the land's **Emblem**, a TM and a reward of the land's own. `PERIODOS.md` has all ten (decision PG-33).

**How it works.**
- The chart has ten panels, one per land. **A panel fills when you win that land's Emblem.** Alethea sets each Emblem into its panel at the *Euploia*'s chart table, and writes what's true and what was only told. The Arimaspians have one eye each (true: they squint); the Pygmies fight cranes (true); the Atarantes have no names (true); the gold-guardians are birds (false: they're bones). Landmarks you've seen add notes, but no panel waits on one.
- The wine-dark sea's panel takes Milo's Tripod Emblem, and its notes come from ODYSSEY's true Periplous.
- At Miletos, Hecataeus himself checks her work and is embarrassed by his own. "I said the Nile flowed from Okeanos. Did it?" "No." "…Put that in."
- **The Scroll of Deeds** tracks the panels, and the trainer card's Oikoumene page shows the Emblems round a disc.
- **The chart's art changes** (stretch). The Oikoumene region map ships with monsters drawn in its unfinished corners. Each completed land wipes its monster off and paints what you saw: a second copy of the chart art, about 15 KiB (§13).

**The end: Herakles at the Pillars.** With all ten Emblems, and `LABORS.md`'s Kotinos won, the Herakleion's priest at Gadeira sends you to the strait. Herakles isn't a hall: he's the capstone. He stands on the northern Pillar you set up for him in Labor 10.

```text
HERAKLES: Twelve labors. Somebody did mine over again, I hear. With a boy from Lakonia.

HERAKLES: You even put my pillars up for me. "No further." I wrote that.

HERAKLES: Nobody listened. Show me why.
```

- **The battle:** Herakles, Lv 86–88, six Pokémon, the Oikoumene's hardest trainer (§6). He isn't a Labor and doesn't overshadow Kleon's: it is a fight with the man whose glory the Labors were measured against, after you've earned your own.
- **After:** he scratches out NE and leaves PLUS ULTRA, "further," and Alethea's chart is finished. In the old games, a man who won all four crowns was a *periodonikes*, a victor of the circuit; Herakles calls you one. Reward: the trainer card's last stamp, the Periodos itself as a wall map in your room at home, and an upgrade to the **War Horn** (SYSTEMS §3, Ares gift II) that rematches every Oikoumene champion daily.

### The rules

1. **One home each.** Every species has a home: the area where it is most common. Areas then fill their remaining slots with "shares" from neighboring areas of the same land and type, so no map feels thin (`COVERAGE.md`, the area table).
2. **The epics' tables add homes, never remove them.** LABORS's Thrace, Themiskyra, Caucasus and far-west maps and ODYSSEY's sea and islands already have their own tables with existing species (LABORS §3; ODYSSEY §4 and §6). `COVERAGE.md` marks those areas as the epics' and lists the homes it adds to them; every species has a home whatever those tables hold.
3. **Evolved forms in the wild.** BALANCE.md's ruling holds: at levels 60–80 a line appears as the stage it reaches by plain level-up. Butterfree is wild; Caterpie is in a Nursery or bred. Lines that evolve another way (stones, trade, friendship, moves) appear at the last stage before that, and the player evolves them.
4. **Type and myth.** A species goes where its types live first (`TYPE_HOMES` in the script). About 290 species are placed by hand for a myth (the `OVERRIDES` table): Apollo Smintheus's mice at Chryse; Pharaoh Proteus's seal-herd and stray Ditto on Pharos (`PERIODOS.md` §5); the cats of Bubastis and the sacred crocodiles in Egypt; the gold-digging ants and their anteaters on the Arimaspian heights; Herodotus's salt hills full of Garganacl; the Hercules beetle on Erytheia; the scarab rolling the sun on the Pyramid Field; Ludicolo on ODYSSEY's Lotus Isle; Stonjourner as the Hyperboreans' walking round temple.
5. **The user's examples are the backbone:** Ice in the north; desert Ground and Psychic in Egypt and Libya; dragons in Colchis; Fairy and Grass on the Isles of the Blessed; Ghost in the Cimmerian mist; Fighting and Steel at Troy.

## 10. The region map in the engine, and a feasibility note

**The short version: the expansion can run two charts, as it does for Hoenn and Kanto with the Sevii Islands. The Oikoumene takes Kanto's *chart slot* and gets its *section IDs* from the Hoenn sections the hoenn-cut session is freeing.** Feasibility: good. The work is data and art, plus about six small code edits.

### What changed on main

The revised Aethos map (`AETHOS_MAP.md`, "Section IDs") needed 83 sections, so its 50 routes and islands now sit on the Kanto block's IDs 89–138, with the Kanto names kept as aliases in `include/constants/region_map_sections_ichor.h`. `GetRegionForSectionId` (`include/regions.h`) now reads that block as Kanto only in an FRLG build; everything else is Hoenn. So:
- the Kanto block is no longer free for the Oikoumene (53 IDs of it remain, by AETHOS_MAP's count);
- nothing in the Emerald build is "Kanto" any more, which leaves the Kanto chart slot unused.

### What the engine does today

| Piece | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Regions | `include/constants/regions.h` | An `enum Region` with `REGION_KANTO`, `REGION_HOENN` and the rest |
| Region of a section | `GetRegionForSectionId`, `include/regions.h` | Ichor's version: Kanto only for the Kanto block in an FRLG build; Hoenn otherwise |
| Map art per chart | `gRegionMapInfos[]`, `src/region_map.c` around line 329 | One entry per `enum RegionMapType` (`REGION_MAP_HOENN`, `_KANTO`, `_SEVII123`, `_SEVII45`, `_SEVII67`): graphics, tilemap and palette for the Fly map and the Pokédex area view |
| Which chart to show | `GetRegionMapType`, `src/region_map.c` line 1168 | Picks the chart from the region of the player's current section, then Kanto's sub-chart |
| Section grid per chart | `GetMapSecIdAt`, around line 1190 | Reads the Kanto grid (`region_map_layout_kanto.h`) for the Kanto region, Hoenn's for the rest |
| Fly targets | `sFlyLocations[]`, line 2186 | Each entry has a `regionMapType`; `CreateFlyDestIcons` (line 2385) draws only the current chart's |
| Battle music by region | `src/pokemon.c` around line 5168 | Kanto trainers get a different theme |
| Met locations | `metloc_u8_t`, `include/gametypes.h` | A byte: every section ID must stay below `0xFD` |

### The plan (decision PG-11)

1. **Section IDs from Hoenn.** Hoenn's main block is IDs 0–86 (Littleroot Town to the Secret Base; 87 is `MAPSEC_DYNAMIC`, which stays). Once the hoenn-cut session frees them, the Oikoumene's sections take that block. It needs about 75 (§11), which leaves a dozen spare, and Kanto's remaining 53 stay spare for either chart.
   - **Contiguous on purpose,** so one range check decides the chart.
   - **Aliases, as AETHOS_MAP did.** `region_map_sections_ichor.h` keeps every reused Hoenn name defined as an alias for its Oikoumene section, so upstream code that names Hoenn places still compiles.
   - **The audit.** Upstream names Hoenn sections in about 250 places across 14 files (most in `src/region_map.c`, which Ichor already rewrites, and `src/landmark.c`; a handful in `event_object_movement.c`, `follower_helper.c`, `overworld.c`, `decoration.c`, `frontier_pass.c`, `pokedex_area_screen.c`, `dexnav.c` and `battle_tent.c`). Any that would fire on an Oikoumene place must be cut or guarded first. That's the hoenn-cut session's grep, and it gates this plan.
2. **The chart slot.** Add `#define REGION_OIKOUMENE REGION_KANTO` in `include/constants/regions.h`, and one line at the top of `GetRegionForSectionId`: a section from `MAPSEC_OIKOUMENE_FIRST` to `MAPSEC_OIKOUMENE_LAST` returns `REGION_OIKOUMENE`. Every piece above then follows: `GetRegionMapType` returns `REGION_MAP_KANTO`, the Kanto grid holds the Oikoumene's cells, fly targets filter by chart, and the Pokédex area view loads the Oikoumene art.
3. **The chart art.** Replace `graphics/pokenav/region_map/map_kanto.png` (and the Pokédex copy) with the Oikoumene disc. Extend `tools/ichor/art/ui_world_map.py` to paint it and write `region_map_layout_kanto.h` and its section names, so art, grid and names stay in step, as they do for Aethos.
4. **The crossing targets.** One fly target per chart for the other (§2): AETHOS on the Oikoumene's center, THE OIKOUMENE in Aethos's edge fog. Each is a section, an `sFlyLocations` entry and a heal location pointing across. **Low.**
5. **Music.** Point the region branch at Ichor's Oikoumene trainer theme. **One line.**
6. **Map-name popups.** Hoenn-block sections can have their own popup themes (`src/map_name_popup.c`'s table covers IDs below the Kanto block), so each land can get its own frame: papyrus for Egypt, felt for the steppe. **Low.**
7. **Sevii as sub-charts, if needed.** `GetKantoSubregion` (`src/regions.c`) lists which sections belong to Sevii 1–3, 4–5 and 6–7, each with its own art. Listing Oikoumene sections there gives a zoomed inset: the Western Sea or Okeanos, if the main disc runs out of tiles. Optional.

### The tile limit, checked

- **Each chart has its own budget.** The Oikoumene's art is its own `gRegionMapInfos` entry, so the 232-tile Pokédex limit and the 256-tile Fly-map limit apply to it separately from Aethos's 186.
- **The estimate:** Aethos fits 83 sections in 186 tiles using four techniques (marching-squares coast, whole-tile islands, tile-local stamps, waves only on open sea). The Oikoumene disc needs about 75 sections. Its likely cost: Okeanos's ring band, about 24 tiles (no flipped tiles on an affine background, so each curve and corner is its own); coasts, about 80; deserts, steppe, ice and mountains, about 25; town and landmark glyphs, about 40; the Aethos inlay, about 12; routes and lanes, about 15; fills, about 6. **About 200 tiles: under 232, with some room.**
- **If it runs over,** the script stops at 232 (AETHOS_MAP, Limits). Two levers: draw the Aethos inlay as a single plain shape, or move the Western Sea's islands to a Sevii-style inset (point 7).
