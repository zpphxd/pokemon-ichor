# Nostail, Melodrakon and the Hydra line: concepts

Three design directions each for the post-game beasts:

- **Nostail** (#138, Normal): Argos, Odysseus's hound. See `species/argos.yaml`.
- **Melodrakon** (#139, Dragon/Fairy): Ladon, the serpent of the golden apples. See `species/ladon.yaml`.
- **The Hydra line** (#140–142, Dragon/Poison): Lernip, Trilerna and Lernathan. See `species/hydra.yaml`.

All of them are in the legendary tier. Each concept is a 64×64 battle front facing left, at about 70% finish. Each Hydra direction is drawn for all three stages, so the line reads as one creature growing. The comparison sheet is `sheets/beasts.png`. It shows each species or line as a row at 3x and 1x, beside official sprites for scale.

The rule for every direction is that the signature motif is part of the anatomy, never a prop:

- the sea is Nostail's coat
- the hood is Melodrakon's canopy
- the hood, fan or fork is how the Hydra's heads are arranged

Every face is an animal or serpent face, with no human features and no rows of teeth.

## Nostail

All three keep the brief's dog: an animal muzzle with a nose pad, a marble-gray muzzle for age, sun-gold eyes and the worn terracotta collar with a black-figure key. They differ in pose, which carries the myth: waiting, recognising, and running home.

### A: The Sea-Watch (`nostail_a.png`)

A seated sighthound sentinel in the pose of a temple dog, watching the sea for twenty years. The sea is written on its back: a deep Aegean saddle whose edge breaks into the Greek wave scroll. Art historians call that pattern the "running dog", so the pun is the point. Its ears stream back like a crest, and its tail wraps the paws and rises as a breaking wave, the tip lifted mid-wag, with sun-gold spray on the crests.

- Silhouette: tall and vertical, with an ear streamer above and a wave curl below.
- Glow: sun-gold eyes and gold spray on the wave crests.
- Size: 46×57.

### B: The Twenty Lamps (`nostail_b.png`)

The old hound of the brief, lying at the gate with its head lifted, ears dropped and plume tail raised mid-wag. It is dark umber, with a white ruff and a gray muzzle. Twenty sun-gold lights run down its spine from brow to tail tip, one for each year it waited. They read as a road home traced on its back, or a row of lamps left burning for the one who sailed away. This is the mysterious, loyal reading.

- Silhouette: long and low, with the great tail arc rising behind.
- Glow: the twenty lamps.
- Size: 60×57.

### C: Swift and Shining (`nostail_c.png`)

The Greek name Argos means both "swift" and "shining white", and this is the hound as it ran in its youth, or as it runs still. A white sighthound in a flying gallop, with Aegean shadows and long ears streaming like pennants. Its wag throws a comet's arc of sun-gold light over its back. This is the most dynamic of the three, but its Greek iconography is the thinnest: only the collar and the reading of its name.

- Silhouette: a rising diagonal leap.
- Glow: the gold comet arc.
- Size: 61×57.

### Pick: A

- It is the most cohesive. The sea, the waves and the wag are all its own coat and tail.
- It is the most Greek. The wave scroll on its back is the vase pattern actually named after a running dog.
- It reads instantly at 1x: a cream hound on a blue curl.
- The seated pose says loyalty without a word, and the shiny's silver-white coat still works against the blue saddle.

**B is the runner-up.** It has the strongest story and the most mystery, and its twenty lamps could be lifted into A as gold glints along the saddle edge if the user wants both.

## Melodrakon

All three share one serpent head: a gold skull with a horned brow ridge, a pink Fairy eye and a forked pink tongue for the hundred hissing voices. They share sun-gold scales with a terracotta belly, and golden apples with a marble shine. They differ in what the apples grow from.

### A: The Tree That Hisses (`melodrakon_a.png`)

The guardian has grown into the tree it guards. Its coils are the roots and its body spirals up on itself into the trunk. Its cobra hood spreads into the canopy: olive leaf-scales on gold hood-ribs, with the golden apples hanging from the rim. More apples ripen on sprigs along the coils, and a frill of pink-tipped leaves sits behind the jaw. This is the brief's reading, pushed until the snake and the tree are one body.

- Silhouette: a tree, with a round crown on a twisted trunk.
- Glow: sun-gold apples against dark leaves.
- Size: 57×59.

### B: The Evening Halo (`melodrakon_b.png`)

The Hesperides were the nymphs of evening, and their garden lay where the sun sets. Melodrakon rears in a long S with a great frill behind its head like the setting sun: pink at the heart, deepening to rose and dusk at the rim. A golden apple ripens at the tip of every spine, a crown of fruit, and the spines are the hundred voices. A leaf tips its tail. This is the most legendary-looking of the three: a halo, a flowing body and the touchstone's Lugia line.

- Silhouette: a disc ringed with apples over an S-curve.
- Glow: sunset rose with sun-gold apples.
- Size: 54×61.

### C: The Orchard Wings (`melodrakon_c.png`)

A drakon whose wings are boughs. Gold finger-branches carry olive-dark leaves for a membrane, apple blossom along the arms supplies the Fairy pink, and golden apples hang from the wingtips. The near wing sweeps up and back, and the far wing rises over the head.

- Silhouette: a serpent under a V of wings.
- Glow: blossom pink with gold apples.
- Size: 59×60.

### Pick: B

- It is the most mysterious and the most elegant: one flowing S under one great disc.
- The myth is the anatomy. The sunset frill makes it the guardian of the garden of evening, and the apples grow from its spines.
- It reads at 1x as a crowned serpent.

**A is the runner-up**, and the most faithful to the brief: no other Pokémon has a tree silhouette, and "its coils are the trunk" is literally true. **C** is the most conventional dragon; its wings are the least original idea of the three.

## The Hydra line

The heads came first in every direction. Each head is a serpent-dragon head with a violet crest, a gold eye and a small fang. A few heads hiss with the mouth open; none shows rows of teeth. Four rules keep the crowd readable:

- **Clear necks.** Every neck is its own S-curve and meets its skull from behind, so no neck pierces a head.
- **Depth.** Back heads are darker. Near heads are lighter, with pale throat stripes.
- **Direction.** Heads face their own way: up, down, outward.
- **The gold head.** The immortal golden head is the same head grown larger, with a long crest, calm marble eyes and a golden neck. It always rises highest.

Each Lernip has young proportions: a big head, a big eye, a scrap of eggshell and a freckle of gold on its brow, which is the immortal head not yet awake. Each Trilerna has a gold bud on the brow of its middle head. Violet poison spots and dripping venom run through all three directions.

### A: The Fan (`lernip_a.png`, `trilerna_a.png`, `lernathan_a.png`)

The classic Lernaean hydra of the vase painters: a heavy, sprawling marsh lizard, its heads rising from the shoulders on long necks that open like a fan. The violet dorsal spines run down to a spade at the tail tip.

- **Lernip** stands on four stubby legs, with two lumps on its neck where the next heads are waiting.
- **Trilerna** has three heads. One shoulder is a fresh stump with two tiny heads budding from it.
- **Lernathan** has seven heads: three fan back to the right and look away, three fan to the left, and the golden head arcs up through the middle.
- **Sizes:** 39×33, 53×55 and 60×62.

### B: The Hooded Crown (`lernip_b.png`, `trilerna_b.png`, `lernathan_b.png`)

A limbless serpent that rears like a cobra. Its extra heads grow on the rim of its hood. The necks are the hood's ribs, webbed together with skin that glows venom-violet at the heart, so the whole crowd of heads reads as one shape. Heavy coils sit underneath. This direction solves the clutter by turning many heads into a single crowned silhouette.

- **Lernip** is a coiled snakelet. Two hood buds flank its neck on a small violet web.
- **Trilerna** has a three-headed hood.
- **Lernathan** has a spade-shaped hood rimmed with six heads, three to a side. Its golden neck is the hood's spine and its golden head is the crown.
- **Sizes:** 33×32, 47×49 and 56×61.

### C: The Branching (`lernip_c.png`, `trilerna_c.png`, `lernathan_c.png`)

"Cut off one head and two grow back", worn as anatomy. This is an upright, heavy-legged marsh dragon whose single neck forks like a tree and then forks again. Every fork is a healed cut, sealed with a seam of violet regrowth-light. The doubling is visible in the line itself: one neck, then three, then a branching candelabrum.

- **Lernip** stands upright with a forked bud at the base of its neck.
- **Trilerna** has a trunk that splits into three. A cut stump on one branch has two heads budding from it.
- **Lernathan** has five heads, not seven. The golden stem rises in the middle and each side branch forks in two. I chose five so the forks stay legible at 1x.
- **Sizes:** 36×35, 55×54 and 62×62.

### Pick: B

- It is the most original silhouette. No other hydra, and no Pokémon, is a crowned cobra whose crown is made of heads.
- It solves the head problem structurally rather than by spacing: the six heads share one hood, the gold head crowns it, and the violet hood-glow gives the line its signature color.
- The growth reads across the line: buds, then a three-head hood, then the full crown.
- It gives Olympias, the Serpent Queen, a serpent that looks crowned.

**A is the runner-up.** It is the most immediately "hydra", and its 1-3-7 growth is the easiest to read, but seven separate necks are the hardest to keep clean in the final pass. **C** is the cleanest at 1x and has the best myth mechanic, but an upright biped holding out branches drifts toward Hydreigon's arm-heads.

## Strongest overall

**Melodrakon B, then Nostail A.** Of the Hydra concepts, Lernathan B has the most legendary presence.

## Notes for the render pass

- **Palettes.** Every concept uses at most 15 colors, all multiples of 8.
  - The Hydra concepts share one 15-color palette: four greens, two limestone tones, three violets, three golds, a mouth red and white.
  - Nostail A uses cream, Aegean, terracotta and gold. B uses umber, marble, terracotta and gold. C uses white, Aegean shadow, terracotta and gold.
- **The golden head.** It is the family head recolored and enlarged, to keep the line's language consistent. It deserves its own hand-drawn design in the render pass: a calmer, more regal face that is still a serpent's.
- **Hand finishing.** Faces, the wave scroll, the apples and the hood glow are hand-placed on top of code-rendered shapes. The bodies still need the hand-finishing pass: muscle and scale clusters, and cleaner shading on the coils and legs.
- **Not drawn yet.** Frame 2, the back, the follower and the icon views.
- **Edges.** A few sprites reach row 62 or column 1–62. All stay inside the frame.
- **Generator scripts.** They are in `/private/tmp/concepts_beasts/` and may be cleared, so start the final render from the PNGs, not from the scripts.
  - Shared helpers: `kit.py`, `hydra_kit.py` and `mhead.py`.
  - One script per concept: `nost_a.py` (with `nost_a_fin.py`), `nost_b.py`, `nost_c.py`, `melo_a.py`, `melo_b.py`, `melo_c.py`, `hyd_a.py`, `hyd_b.py` and `hyd_c.py`.
  - `write_out.py` writes the PNGs and the sheet.
