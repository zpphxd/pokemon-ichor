## 0. The shape

The main story ends on Olympus. The post-game opens the world in every direction from there.

```text
                               UP
                    Olympus's true summit
              (the Primordials, then the Prime Mover)
                                |
   WEST                         |                          EAST
   the wine-dark sea (Odyssey)  |      Thrace, Themiskyra, the Caucasus
   the Pillars, Erytheia,  ---  AETHOS  ---  (Labors 8, 9, 11)
   the Garden (Labors 10, 11)   |
   beyond: ATLANTIS             |
                                |
                    OLYMPIA, at the centre:
              the Olympiad and the Labors' hub
                                |
                              DOWN
                Cape Tainaron and the Underworld
                          (Katabasis)
```

- **The centre is Olympia,** in Elis on the mainland's west: the Olympiad (the Battle Frontier) and the Heraion, where the Labors are set.
- **Down** is the Katabasis. **West** is the Odyssey's sea, and beyond the Pillars, Atlantis. **East** is the Labors' far lands. **Up** is the end.
- **The known world grows.** TERMS flags that "National Dex" is anachronistic and suggests *Oikoumene*, "the known world". This proposal adopts it, and BALANCE.md names the second map the Oikoumene too: when the post-game opens, the dex's second mode becomes the **Oikoumene Dex**, and the existing 1,025 species start arriving (dex D5).

### What the post-game is about

STORY.md asks one question of the post-game: **"What do you do with an unwritten life?"** (STORY §1, how each act tests it). Its spine gives this file's job as **the world learning to live with an open future** (STORY §3). Prophecy is over; death isn't (STORY §1, rule 9). Each epic takes one piece of that:

| Epic | Its question | Its answer, in play | Whose arc |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Labors | What is glory, if nobody's fated to it? | Glory earned, not fated: Kleon earns his by helping people, and finishes three behind | **Kleon** |
| The Katabasis | What happens to death without a schedule, and to "for ever"? | Death is still real and still right. The last fixed punishments end, and the first Archon hears "It wasn't the oracle. I chose." | **The Cutter, and Medon** |
| The Odyssey | Glory or home? | The long way home: Kleon chose glory, and you choose the homecoming | **Alethea, and Mom** |
| Atlantis | Is every myth a memory of a Pokémon? | Even the one a philosopher made up | **Daphne** |
| The Olympiad | Who are the heroes, without prophecies? | The ones who show up and compete | The heroes |
| The Primordials and the Prime Mover | What was there before the threads? | The world itself; then the thing that moves it | **The player** |

Every epic keeps STORY §3's three rules: no one reads the future; augurs read the weather (SYSTEMS §6); the Pythia does "Know thyself" (SYSTEMS §11).

### 3.2 Atlantis: Daphne's arc

> *Beyond the Pillars of Herakles lay an island larger than Libya and Asia together, with a city built in rings of land and sea around Poseidon's temple. In a single day and night it sank* (Plato, *Timaeus* 24e–25d; *Critias* 113c–121c).

**The hook.** Professor Daphne has spent her life on one idea: every myth is a memory of a Pokémon. But one myth she's never believed is Atlantis, because Plato made it up to make a point about Athens. "Every myth but that one." Then Tiresias, in the Meadows of Asphodel, remembers "a city under the sea that sank in a day" (UNDERWORLD §4.7), and the Pillars of Herakles are standing at the end of the sea (LABORS, Labor 10). Daphne asks to come aboard the *Euploia*. She walked off her island at Eleusa (STORY §2, Daphne), and she's been walking since. This is her first voyage off the edge of the map.

**Unlock.** Labor 10's Pillars set, and the trireme (from the Odyssey's first act, available from the start of the post-game). Tiresias's line is a seed, not a gate. Atlantis is in BALANCE.md's **Aethos post-game row** (bosses up to 80), so it doesn't wait for the Odyssey, which is in the Oikoumene's (§2).

**The arc in outline.**
1. **Beyond the Pillars.** The outer ocean, past the strait: the largest sea map, open water, long swells, no islands. The fog parts with Aeolus's Bag.
2. **The Nereids.** Fifty sea-nymphs on dolphins ring a patch of water that glows. Their leader is **Thetis**, Achilles's mother. They give you the **Nereid Pearl** (SYSTEMS §1, relic 12: Dive) after a double battle against two of them. If you've won Achilles's silver symbol, Thetis mentions her son: "Tell him to eat something."
3. **The rings.** Plato's city is concentric rings of land and water around a central island. Each ring is an underwater map you reach by diving.
   - **The signature mechanic is breath.** Underwater, the Nereid Pearl gives you **60 steps** of breath on Hero (80 on Mortal, 45 on Spartan), shown as a counter. Air pockets under the old domes and bubble vents on the sea floor refill it. Running out isn't death: you float to the last air pocket (fail forward). It's the carried-flame grammar again (SYSTEMS §2), turned into air.
   - **The outer ring:** the harbor, full of orichalcum wrecks.
   - **The middle ring:** palaces and the racecourse Plato describes.
   - **The inner ring:** the citadel, with the orichalcum pillar where the kings inscribed their laws. The Telchines, Poseidon's smiths (Telpup and Telkanvil, dex #85–86), still work its forges.
4. **The temple.** At the centre, Poseidon's drowned temple, where **Pontanax**, the sea colossus (dex #150), sleeps behind a seal that only the **trident lot** opens (dex D4, below).
5. **The nursery.** In a coral garden off the inner ring, a single Manaphy egg (PITCH: Manaphy in Atlantis). It hatches into the sea's prince.
6. **The abyss.** Below the city, a trench. The white bird of the Odyssey's storm, Leukothea, is **Lugia**, and she waits at its bottom (Lv 80).
7. **The edge of the world.** Past the abyss runs the river Oceanus, the world-ocean that circles the earth: **Kyogre**, the first Primordial (§3.3).
8. **Daphne's last lecture.** On the deck, after: "So Plato was remembering something too. Even when he thought he was making it up." The Cetus constellation is placed (SYSTEMS §8, #23).

**Also here:**
- **The other fossil.** The dex says the fossil you didn't pick at Knossa "turns up in Atlantis". It's in the middle ring's museum hall, and Daphne revives it.
- **Charybdis's Hoard** (ODYSSEY §6.7) becomes divable with the Pearl.
- **Briareus,** if you beat him rather than caught him, waits at sea off the Pillars (UNDERWORLD §4.10).
- **Proteus** (Haligeron) in the shallows (dex habitat).

**Maps** (about 12): the outer ocean, the Nereids' circle, three rings (two maps each: a surface ruin and an underwater ring), the temple, the nursery, the abyss, the edge of the world. **ROM: about 280 KiB** (an `atlantis` underwater tileset, about 40 KiB, the rest as LABYRINTH's ratios). INVENTORY §2 already lists the player's underwater sheets for Phase 5.

**Wild** (Lv 65–70, Aethos species only; existing species live in the Oikoumene, §4): Telpup and Telkanvil (Poseidon's smiths), Haligeron, Kynoketos and Ketofry, Aphrell and Aphrogene, Narkeray, Karkipool and Karkitox, Dakrock, Galenyon, and the fossil lines Monophant and Sepidart in the ruins (rare, after the museum's revival).

### 3.3 The Primordials and the Prime Mover

> *First came Chaos, the Gap; then broad-breasted Gaia, the Earth; misty Tartaros in her depths; and Eros. Gaia bore starry Ouranos, the Sky, to cover her on every side. Their son Kronos cut his father down with an adamant sickle* (Hesiod, *Theogony* 116–182).

**The hook.** The Harpe, the adamant sickle the whole main story was about, is the blade that first separated Earth and Sky. You held it. The Primordials know.

**The three.** The bible casts Groudon as Gaia, Kyogre as Oceanus and Rayquaza as Ouranos (PITCH §6.6). Each wakes in its own domain, and each needs its epic first.

| Primordial | Species | Where | Wakes after | The scene | Lv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gaia**, the Earth | Groudon | Under Delphoi, down through the Korykian cave to the first oracle: Delphi was Gaia's before it was Apollo's, and the Omphalos is her navel | The Katabasis (emptying Tartaros, who lies in her depths, wakes her) | The cave heats; the sun comes out underground | 88 |
| **Oceanus**, the world-river | Kyogre | The edge of the world, below Atlantis | Atlantis | A river that runs in a circle, forever; rain from nowhere | 88 |
| **Ouranos**, the Sky | Rayquaza | Above Olympus's clouds, past Pegaunos's cloud meadows | The Kotinos: Herakles's labors were his road up to Olympus, and yours open the way past it | He remembers the sickle. He comes down to look at the one who held it. | 88 |

- **The Hoenn set pieces return,** re-themed: INVENTORY §10 lists `src/rayquaza_scene.c`, the weather trio's songs and the Cave of Origin and Sealed Chamber songs (about 336 KiB already in the ROM) as "re-theme, not cut". The Rayquaza descent scene becomes Ouranos coming down; the Groudon and Kyogre weather (harsh sun and heavy rain) stay as their battle weather.
- **Rayquaza is one of three, not the centre.** LAZARUS.md tells us to avoid Rayquaza as the central legendary. Here it is the third of three Primordials, and Arceus is above all of them.
- **Ichor's own primordial is Giratina,** as Tartaros (UNDERWORLD §4.11), and it's already met in the Katabasis. Hesiod's four firsts are Chaos, Gaia, Tartaros and Eros: Ichor gives Tartaros to Giratina and Gaia to Groudon. Chaos is the Gap that Arceus fills (below). Eros is the dex's Himerow line and Enamorus, at Kythera.

**The Prime Mover.**

> *There is something that moves without being moved, eternal. It moves the others as the object of love moves the lover* (Aristotle, *Metaphysics* Λ 7).

**The summit.** Olympus's true summit, above the Pantheon and above the clouds where Ouranos lives. The path opens with **four keys**, one from each epic, set into a stone at the summit's foot:

**The mechanic: the unmoved mover.** At the very top, Arceus stands in a field of light and does not move.

- **So the last puzzle in the game is to stop.** Put the controller down. Wait. (A message after the third failed walk: "Some things you can't reach by going to them.")

## 6. Save data

SYSTEMS §14 puts all of Ichor's new state in one struct, `IchorSave`, in SaveBlock3: about 760 of 1,624 bytes are used. The post-game adds one sub-struct.

```c
struct IchorEpics              // POSTGAME.md; 64 bytes
{
    // LABORS.md §12
    u16 laborsSet;             // 12 bits
    u16 laborsDone;            // 12 bits
    u16 laborsDedicated;       // 12 bits
    u8  laborStep[12];         // per-Labor state
    u32 lernaPools;            // Labor 2: nine 2-bit pools + the moved-fire bit
    // UNDERWORLD.md §13
    u8  titans;                // 6 bits
    u16 shades;                // shades met, unburied paid for
    u8  oathAtRest;
    // ODYSSEY.md §15
    u16 crew;                  // 10 bits
    u8  oarsmenTaken;          // Scylla's toll and the rescued bits
    u8  wind;                  // direction, strength, grudge, becalmed
    u16 storm;                 // Tornadus's position while loose
    u16 voyage;                // the voyage's choices
    u8  shipUpgrades;
    // POSTGAME.md
    u16 kleos;                 // the twelve laurels
    u8  colossusLots;          // 3 bits held, 3 bits used
    u8  primordials;           // Gaia, Oceanus, Ouranos, the Prime Mover
    u8  atlantisBreath;        // carried between dives on the same visit
    u8  version;               // for migrations
    u8  pad[5];
};
```

- **It fits:** 64 bytes, taking `IchorSave` to about 824 of 1,624.
- **Vars:** three new ones, against the 18 left after LABYRINTH's two (SYSTEMS §0 counts 20 unused): `VAR_ICHOR_KATABASIS`, `VAR_ICHOR_ODYSSEY` and `VAR_ICHOR_POSTGAME` (the epilogue's aftermath, Atlantis and the summit, 0–20).
- **Flags:** about 140 across the post-game (LABORS about 40, UNDERWORLD about 30, ODYSSEY about 35, this file about 35). SYSTEMS §14 already uses about 250 of the 332 unused flags. The post-game's come from the **Hoenn story and hidden-item flags that free up as Hoenn's maps are removed** (INVENTORY §9), which number in the hundreds. Phase 5 should claim them only after Phase 4 has removed Hoenn's maps.
- **Add the struct with `IchorSave`,** early in Phase 2, even though it's empty until Phase 5. SYSTEMS §14 warns that changing the save layout breaks saves; adding it once, early, avoids a second break.
