## 0. The hook

Under the ruined palace of Knossa, Daedalus's Labyrinth goes down in seven rings. Each ring is a lesson the player has already learned at one of the seven temples. The player walks it holding Ariadne's red thread, and the thread marks every step. At the bottom waits the Minotaur. He is **Asterion**, "the starry one", a frightened bull the Titans have goaded into a monster. You free him instead of killing him.

Then the healer you trusted takes off a golden mask. The house turns to trap you, and the only way out is the thread you laid. Where it lies, the walls can't close.

Next morning, an old man who lost his son asks you to fly up the shaft his boy flew from, and to come back down.

### Pillars

1. **Seven rings, seven lessons.** Ring I tests the Hestia trial's verb, Ring II Hermes's, and so on to Ring VII and Aphrodite's. The Starry Chamber previews Apollo's beams, the one laurel still to come. The Labyrinth is the final exam of every temple so far.
2. **The thread is the dungeon.**
   - You lay it, and you wind it back in when you retrace your steps.
   - You tie it off at bronze rings to save your place.
   - In the end it saves you.
3. **The monster who isn't.** Asterion is first a threat, then a tool (his charge breaks walls), then an ally. Nobody is fated to be what they're made into.
4. **Two fathers.** Daedalus outlived his son. Phaedon will not let his daughter die. Both are clever, gentle men who will break the world to undo a loss. It is the game's theme of hubris in miniature.
5. **Fail forward.**
   - No puzzle here ends in a game over or a lockout.
   - Asterion's charge knocks you back to your last tied anchor, and you keep everything else.
   - Every puzzle has a safety valve.

### Daedalus's seven pages

When Icarus fell, he was carrying his father's book, and the pages blew across the Aegean. Each page holds one ring's secret.

- **Pages are knowledge, never gates.** Every secret exists without its page; the page tells you where.
- You can read a page in the bag at any time, as a sketch with three lines of notes.
- Several pages need relics to reach, so the relics feed the Labyrinth.

**The Minoan look,** for the `knossa` tileset (§11):
- red columns that **taper downward**, wider at the top, with black capitals;
- horns of consecration on the roof lines;
- double-axe marks cut into gypsum blocks;
- light wells;
- frescoes: dolphins, lilies, bull-leapers, the lily prince, saffron gatherers, blue monkeys and griffins. Griffins are fine as painted décor. Only "Gryphon"-prefixed *forms* are on the Lazarus avoid-list.

**The palace's three scenes:**
1. **The West Magazines.** The Titan camp among pithoi taller than you. Overheard grunt talk starts the Ring IV password trail (§6, Ring IV).
2. **The Throne Room.** Ariadne (scene A). She sends you to the Court: "Can you leap a bull? If you can leap a bull, you can understand one."
3. **The Central Court.** Bull-leaping, below. One clean leap is needed for the story, and the minigame is built so nobody fails it for long.

### Bull-leaping (the *taurokathapsia*)

**Play.**
1. The Gentle Bull, a calm Tauros, paws at the far end of a 9-tile lane. You stand at the near end.
2. He charges. A painted line on the paving marks the tile in front of you.
3. Press A as his head crosses the line. You grip the horns and somersault over him, landing behind.

**Rules.**
| | Mortal | Hero | Spartan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull speed | 1 tile per 8 frames | 1 tile per 6 frames | 1 tile per 5 frames |
| Timing window | 14 frames | 10 frames | 6 frames |

- **Too early:** you jump short and land in front of him. "Too soon!" No harm done, and the bull trots back.
- **Too late:** you are bowled over: a fade, "Ooof." No damage.
- **After each miss** the window grows by 2 frames, up to double. Sarpedon slows the bull down: "Again. Slower, old friend."
- **Prizes** for consecutive leaps in one session:
  | Leaps | Prize |
  |---|---|
  | 3 | Olive Oil ×5 |
  | 5 | TM Body Slam |
  | 10 | the **Leaper's Sash**, a held item: the holder can't be made to flinch |
  | 20 | a Tauros of the palace herd, with its hidden ability and a rare move |
- **The story needs one leap.** The climax needs three over Asterion (§6, the heart), under the same rules.

**Engine.**
- A C special, `Ichor_BullLeap`, runs a task shaped like the fishing minigame's wait-for-A step (`src/fishing.c`, `Fishing_WaitForA`).
- It moves the bull object one tile at a time with fast walk actions, measures the A press against the bull's tile, and returns `VAR_RESULT` (0 early, 1 clean, 2 late).
- The vault is the two-tile ledge jump (`jump_2`) with the player turning through four facings mid-air, a cheap somersault.
- **Low–Medium.**

## 5. The mechanics

### 5.1 Ariadne's Thread

**Play.**
- From the moment Ariadne puts the spool in your hand, every step you take on the Labyrinth's floor lays a red thread behind you. You can see where you've been.
- **Turn back the way you came and the thread winds itself up,** so a dead end costs you nothing.
- Bronze rings are set in the floor at junctions. Stand on one and **tie off**: the thread so far becomes permanent, the spool refills, and that ring becomes the place you return to if Asterion knocks you down.
- At the climax, the thread you laid is the only path the turning house can't close.

**Rules.**

| | Mortal | Hero | Spartan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spool per segment | 150 tiles | 120 tiles | 100 tiles |

- **Laying is automatic** on threaded maps (the Labyrinth's rings) while the Thread is in the bag. Only floor tiles take thread; stairs and doorways don't.
- **Winding.** Stepping back onto the previous tile of the live segment removes the last tile and returns 1 spool. Crossing an older, tied thread is allowed; the newer thread draws on top.
- **Empty spool.** "The spool is nearly empty" appears at 10 left. At 0 you can still walk, but no thread is laid.
- **Tie off.** Do it from the Relic Ring while standing on an anchor ring. It commits the segment, refills the spool, and makes this your current anchor. Each ring's entrance has an anchor that ties itself.
- **The Thread menu** (Relic Ring → Thread):
  - **TIE OFF**, when you're on a ring.
  - **RETURN TO…**, with a list of tied anchors by name ("Ring III: the workshop door") plus the Grand Staircase. This is a fade warp.
  - **WIND IN**, which clears the live segment at once.
  - **LEAVE.**
  - Outside the Labyrinth, the Thread is simply an escape that returns you to a dungeon's entrance, like the Escape Rope.
- **Knockdowns.** Asterion's charge returns you to your current anchor. The live segment winds in. Lit braziers, opened gates, broken walls and beaten trainers all stay as they are.
- **The Turning** (§7). Committed thread tiles **never** become walls. The RETURN menu is disabled ("The thread shakes in your hand. Walk it.").

**Myth and story.**
- Ariadne gave Theseus a clew of thread, on Daedalus's advice. Here she gives it to someone with no thread of their own: "Daedalus spun it for me when I was small. So I'd stop getting lost."

**Data.** These bytes are part of `IchorSave` in SaveBlock3 (SYSTEMS §14).

```c
struct ThreadAnchor { u16 mapId; u8 x, y; u16 steps; };  // 6 B; steps = length of the segment it starts
struct IchorThread                                       // ~660 B
{
    struct ThreadAnchor anchors[24];  // 144 B: every tied segment starts at an anchor
    u8  anchorCount, currentAnchor;   // 2 B
    u8  committed[448];               // 2 bits per step: 1,792 committed steps
    u16 liveMapId; u8 liveX, liveY;   // where the live segment starts
    u8  liveLen;                      // ≤ 150
    u8  live[38];                     // 2 bits per step: 152 steps
};
```

- **Segments never cross maps.** Ring stairs end one segment, and the next ring's entrance anchor starts another.
- **If `committed` fills,** the oldest segments, from the outer rings, are dropped and their marks disappear on reload. The Turning's fallback still covers those rings, since Asterion breaks the walls (§7). A typical careful run lays about 1,200 committed steps.

**Engine.**
- **The per-step callback.**
  - It follows the Sootopolis Gym ice exactly. `SootopolisGymIcePerStepCallback` in `src/field_tasks.c` (around line 675) watches the player's position, swaps the metatile under them and records the tile in a bitfield.
  - `SetSootopolisGymCrackedIceMetatiles` redraws every recorded tile on load.
  - Ichor adds `STEP_CB_ICHOR_THREAD` to `sPerStepCallbacks` (`include/constants/field_tasks.h`).
- **Drawing.**
  - Each threadable floor metatile has twins in the `labyrinth` tileset: 2 horizontal and vertical straights, 4 corners and 4 end caps.
  - The shape comes from the entry and exit directions. The head tile shows an end cap.
  - To restore a tile, read the original metatile from the map's layout data (`gMapHeader.mapLayout->map`).
  - With 2 base floors, that is 20 twin metatiles.
- **The redraw.** `special Ichor_DrawThread` in each ring's `ON_LOAD` walks the anchors and committed bits for the current map.
- **Effort: Medium,** about 350 lines. The risk is low, because the vanilla ice puzzle proves the pattern.

### 5.2 Darkness, the torch and braziers

The Labyrinth uses SYSTEMS §2 unchanged.

- **Radius.** Without the torch, 24 px; with Hecate's Torch, 56 px; with the Three-Flamed Torch, 64 px.
- **Brazier zones.** Each ring is divided into named zones, and lighting a zone's brazier lights the zone for good (lit zones, SYSTEMS §2). Rings I and III build their puzzles on this.
- **Glow-paint.** Daedalus marked his own routes with a paint that glows. Douse the torch to see it (Ring III).
- **Skylights** (Ring V) are fixed pools of light. They are the only light in the Hunt, and they decide what Asterion can see.

### 5.3 Asterion: AI and stealth rules

Asterion is never fought in the rings. He is a hazard you read, and later a tool you use.

**His states.**

| State | What he does | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| **Patrol** | Walks his loop at walking pace: 16 frames per tile (12 at night in Ring V) | He sees or hears you |
| **Listen** | Heard you but can't see you. Turns toward the sound and stands for 60 frames, ears up. | He sees you (go to Alert), or he resumes his patrol |
| **Alert** | Sees you. A "!" appears, he lowers his horns and scrapes the floor with a dust puff. **You keep control** for 40 frames on Hero (60 Mortal, 30 Spartan). | Charge |
| **Charge** | Runs straight along the line he faced, at 4 frames per tile | He hits you, a wall or a cracked wall |
| **Knockdown** | You are bowled over: a fade, then you wake at your current anchor | He returns to his patrol start |
| **Stunned** | Hit a wall: stars over his head, 120 frames. He can't see or hear. You can walk right past him. | Patrol, from the nearest waypoint |
| **Breakthrough** | Hit a cracked wall: it bursts (rubble, a rumble, and it stays broken), then he is stunned for 60 frames in the gap | Patrol. The new opening stays. |
| **Rest** | The bronze bell (Page V) was rung: he lies down for 30 of your steps | He wakes early if you bump him |

**What he sees.**
- A straight line in the direction he faces, blocked by walls. This is Gen 3 trainer sight.
- The range depends on where **you** stand:

| Where you stand | Range |
|---|---|
| A dark tile | 3 tiles |
| A sunlit skylight pool | 7 tiles |
| A moonlit pool (at night) | 5 tiles |
| A dark tile, wearing the Helm of Darkness | 0: never |
| A pool, wearing the Helm | 2 tiles |

**What he hears.** He checks on each of your steps, measuring distance in steps (Manhattan):

| You are | He hears you within |
|---|---|
| Running (Talaria) | 5 tiles |
| Walking | 2 tiles |
| Running in the Helm | 3 tiles |
| Walking in the Helm | 0: never |

**Difficulty:** on Mortal, sight and hearing are each 1 tile shorter. On Spartan, each is 1 tile longer.

### 5.4 The Helm of Darkness

- **Use.** Put it on or take it off from the Relic Ring. It works **only in darkness** (dark tiles on dark maps). In light, it just makes you harder to see (2 tiles).
- **Look.** The player's sprite turns semi-transparent. The engine already blends sprites, so set the player's object mode to blend while the Helm is on. No new art is needed.
- **Effect on trainers.**
  - With the Helm on, in the dark, trainers and sentries don't spot you. Their sight uses the same light test as Asterion's.
  - You can still talk to them to battle. Beaten and unbeaten trainers keep their flags.
  - The expansion's all-or-nothing `OW_FLAG_NO_TRAINER_SEE` (`include/config/overworld.h`) is the quick version for the dark rings, if the per-tile test slips.
- **Myth.** The Cyclopes forged it for Hades in the war on the Titans. Minos, judge of the dead, was buried with it: "a judge must see without being seen." After the story, in the Katabasis, Minos asks for it back, then lets you keep it (SYSTEMS §16 item 5).

### 5.7 Ariadne's dancing floor (the Choros)

Homer says Daedalus built a dancing floor at Knossos for Ariadne (*Iliad* 18.590–592). Plutarch says Theseus led a dance on Delos, the crane dance, that imitated the Labyrinth's windings.

**The floor** is 5×5 tiles, each painted with a symbol: **X** double axe, **W** wave, **L** lily, **R** rosette, **S** star. The player enters from the south edge. Each step plays a lyre note: X is D, W is E, L is F, R is G, and S is A.

```text
          A    B    C    D    E
     1    S    X    W    R    L
     2    L    R    L    W    R
     3    R    W    S    X    L
     4    R    W    L    W    W
     5    W    X    W    L    X        <- enter from the south
```

**The dance** is X W L R W L R W S. Its notes, D E F G E F G E A, are the motif of the Labyrinth's music, so the player has been hearing the answer since Ring I.

**The one solution.** Moves are orthogonal, and no tile can be stepped on twice:

```text
     E5 (X) -> E4 (W) -> E3 (L) -> E2 (R) -> D2 (W) -> C2 (L) -> B2 (R) -> B3 (W) -> C3 (S)
```

This spirals inward to the star at the centre. There are three false starts:
- B5 (X), then A5 (W), dies at once.
- B5 (X), C5 (W), C4 (L) dies after three steps.
- B5 (X), C5 (W), D5 (L) dies after three steps.

An exhaustive search confirms it: this is the only path that spells the dance, even if the player could start on any tile.

**The clues.** Frescoes in Rings IV, V and VI each show dancers performing two or three steps in order, as poses holding the symbols. Page VII shows the whole dance.

**Failure.** A wrong step plays a sour chord and dims the floor, and you step off and start again. After three failures, Ariadne says "…Fine. Watch my feet." and dances it, then you follow her.

**Engine.** One coord event per tile, a dance-progress var (`VAR_TEMP_1`), and `playse` for each note. **Low.**

## 7. The escape and the dawn

## 8. The cast: arcs and draft scenes

### The arcs

- **Ariadne** is the daughter of Minos, priestess of the Mistress of the Labyrinth, and Asterion's half-sister.
  - **Before:** she let the Titans in because their masked master swore he could mend anything, her brother included.

  - **His fall:** he helped Kronos. He made the collar that binds the Harpe, and timed the hour to finish it by the stars, because a grieving father told him no father would ever bury a child again.
  - **His turn:** watching the Titans drive Asterion through his halls with fire. He gives you the Labrys.
  - **Payoff:** watching you fly the Icarus Shaft and land. With all seven pages, he confesses about Perdix. In the post-game, Talos visits.
  - **Voice:** precise, wry, then suddenly unguarded.
- **Asterion** is a Pokémon. He never speaks.
  - **His arc is behavior:** a terror in Ring II, a tool in Ring V, freed at the heart, an ally in the escape.
  - **Myth:** Pausanias says the Minotaur's name was Asterion. His mother named him before everyone decided what he was. That is the theme: nobody is fated to be what they're made into.
  - **Post-game:** Ariadne's ace, then possibly your partner (§10).
- **Phaedon / Kronos** is the healer at Astyra's Asclepeion.
  - **His daughter Elpis** is fated to die young. He has heard it from the Oracle and from every healer, and read it in her himself.
  - **His motive:** he means to stop the Loom so that no thread is cut.
  - **His tell:** he calls the bull "Starry", after Elpis's toy.
  - **His reveal is a choice.** He takes off the mask himself, because he wants your help at the Loom and would rather ask with his own face.
  - **Voice:** gentle, tired, utterly reasonable. That is why it's frightening.
- **Kleon** is the rival.
  - **His fall:** Iapetos's forged prophecy (Act II) says he'll slay the starry bull, so he hunts Asterion loudly and gets flattened.
  - **At the heart,** he tries to kill the freed bull, and you stop him.
  - **The tablet** in Iapetos's hand sends him storming off to Lakonia (PITCH Act III step 3).
- **Hyperion** is the Titan of light, brash, and the forge's boss. "Roast" is his word (the slice's grunts fear it).
- **Rhea** is the mother "who lost her children to prophecy" (PITCH).
  - She holds Asterion's chain. When ordered to seal the house with you inside, she leaves the threshold open.
  - In myth, Rhea saved her last child from Kronos in a Cretan cave. Here, on Crete, she makes the same choice again.
  - It is the first crack in Team Titan (decision L5).
- **Alethea** stays above ground. She reads the Bronze Heel, the frescoes and the prophecy fragments, and meets you after the escape. Her tic is "Truthfully, …".

## 10. Post-game: the Endless Labyrinth, and Asterion's return

### The Endless Labyrinth

After the story, the house keeps turning. "Daedalus says it's healthier." Ariadne opens it as a challenge.
- It is the **Battle Pyramid engine**, which INVENTORY §10 keeps for the Olympiad, re-skinned.
- It belongs to the Olympiad's circuit but sits at Knossa: the lobby is the Throne Room, and the entrance is the Grand Staircase.

**What the engine already does** (`src/battle_pyramid.c`):

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Floors | Each floor is assembled from a 4×4 grid of 8×8 chunk layouts, chosen from 16 (`GenerateBattlePyramidFloorLayout`, around line 1742; `BattlePyramidSquare01`–`16` in `data/layouts/layouts.json`), into a fresh 32×32 floor |
| Rounds | 7 floors and then the top (`BattlePyramid_WarpToNextFloor` in `data/maps/BattleFrontier_BattlePyramidFloor/scripts.inc`) |
| Light | The radius starts at **32 px** on each floor and grows **4 px per trainer beaten**, up to 120 (`UpdatePyramidLightRadius`, `pyramidLightRadius`) |
| Party and bag | A party of 3 at level 50 or open level; a separate bag of floor-found items, the Pyramid Bag, which becomes **Ariadne's Satchel**. Held items are cleared. |
| Wild Pokémon | Can be generated from the round's rules (`BATTLE_PYRAMID_RANDOM_ENCOUNTERS` in `include/config/overworld.h`) |
| Relics | The registered-item path refuses to run in the Pyramid (`UseRegisteredKeyItemOnField`), so relics are off, as they should be |

**Ichor's changes.**

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| The 16 chunks | Redrawn as Labyrinth corridors, with the frescoes, braziers and pools as décor. The new tileset must keep the Pyramid's exit and floor metatile constants, because the layout code swaps exits for floor by id. |
| **The Brain** | **Ariadne,** "the Mistress of the Labyrinth", at the top of round 3 (the silver **Thread Symbol**) and round 10 (the gold). Her ace is **Asterion**, with a unique set: Raging Bull, Close Combat, Head Smash and Bulk Up; Intimidate, or Anger Point at gold. |
| Asterion as the Brain? | The brief suggested making Asterion himself the Brain. A Pokémon can't run a facility or talk to you, so this proposal makes Ariadne the Brain and Asterion her partner, which keeps the pair together (decision L3). |
| **Bull floors** (stretch) | Every 7th floor has no trainers, but a young bull of the herd patrols, using §5.3's rules. Reach the exit unseen for a bonus prize. It needs the full Asterion AI. |
| Rewards | Olympiad points; at gold, the "Thread" stamp on the trainer card |

**Engine:** **Medium.** Re-skin, replace data tables, edit scripts. The risk is in the Frontier save struct and the Brain logic. Keep Brandon's code paths and swap in Ariadne's data.

## Decisions for the user

These are the Labyrinth's. SYSTEMS.md has D1–D7.

- **L1. Daedalus's complicity.** Daedalus made the Harpe's collar and timed its hour for Kronos, out of sympathy for a grieving father, then repents. The alternative is that Daedalus is purely a victim.
- **L2. Talos was Perdix.** Talos is Daedalus's estranged apprentice, called Perdix as a boy, and the Wings come from him at Laurel 6 (Fly at badge 6, as in Emerald).
- **L3. Ariadne is the Endless Labyrinth's Brain,** with Asterion as her ace, rather than Asterion as the Brain.
- **L4. Asterion joins you** in the post-game, after Ariadne's gold symbol, or stays on Knossa as a friend only.
- **L5. Rhea's mercy** at the heart, as the first crack in Team Titan.
- **L6. Kleon at Knossa.** He hunts Asterion because of the forged prophecy, gets flattened in Ring V, and is your rival battle at the heart.
- **L7. The unmasking is Phaedon's choice.** He unmasks to ask for your help, after the "Starry" tell, rather than being exposed.
- **L8. The two-line slice seed** in the Titan tablet.
- **L9. The disguise sprite:** two new player sheets, or keep the disguise as a mechanic with no new art.
- **L10. Scope.** Seven rings, about 3 hours (recommended). The fallback is five rings: fold Ring II into Ring I and Ring VII into the heart, saving about 40 minutes and 2 maps.
